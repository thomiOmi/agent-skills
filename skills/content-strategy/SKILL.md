# Content Strategy

Guidelines for creating effective, audience-focused content.

---

## Examples

**Before (vague, no audience):**

> "Our platform helps businesses streamline their workflow."

**After (specific, audience-focused, value-driven):**

> "Stop chasing receipts. AutoCat logs every mileage, categorizes every expense, and generates your tax report in one click. Built for freelancers who bill by the mile."

---

## Core Principles

1. **Know the audience** -  write for a specific reader, not everyone
2. **Clear over clever** -  clarity always wins over wordplay
3. **Value first** -  every piece of content must provide measurable value
4. **Consistent voice** -  brand tone is consistent across all channels
5. **Measurable goals** -  every campaign has a clear success metric

---

## SEO Checklist

```
- [ ] Primary keyword in title tag (front-loaded)
- [ ] Meta description under 160 chars with keyword and CTA
- [ ] URL is short and includes primary keyword
- [ ] H1 contains primary keyword (only one H1)
- [ ] H2-H3 structure with secondary keywords where natural
- [ ] Image alt text includes relevant keywords
- [ ] Internal links to 2-3 related pages
- [ ] External links to 1-2 authoritative sources
- [ ] Content is comprehensive (covers topic fully, not thin)
- [ ] Mobile-friendly formatting (short paragraphs, bullet points)
```

## Content Types

**Blog post:** Problem to context to solution to key takeaways to CTA
**Landing page:** Headline to value prop to proof to benefits to CTA
**Email:** Subject line to greeting to single message to CTA to closing
**Documentation:** What is it to how to use to examples to API reference
**Social:** Hook to value to link/visual to CTA (keep under character limit)

## Brand Voice Guidelines

- Define tone: professional, casual, technical, friendly, authoritative
- Define words to use and words to avoid
- Define sentence length preference (short, medium, varied)
- Include examples of on-brand vs off-brand phrasing

---

## Hard Rules

- No false or unverifiable claims
  - Why: False claims destroy trust instantly and can lead to legal liability (false advertising, defamation). If you cannot cite a source, rewrite the claim.
- No jargon without explanation (unless writing for technical audience)
  - Why: Unexplained jargon excludes readers who are not domain experts. For sales and marketing content, every unexplained term is a lost conversion.
- No clickbait headlines -  headline must deliver what it promises
  - Why: Clickbait drives short-term clicks but destroys long-term trust. Readers who feel misled will not return and may actively warn others.
- No plagiarism -  always attribute and link to sources
  - Why: Plagiarism violates copyright law, damages brand reputation, and penalizes SEO (Google demotes duplicate content). Attribution builds authority.
- Every content piece has a clear target audience and goal
  - Why: Content written for "everyone" resonates with no one. Without a specific audience and measurable goal, you cannot evaluate whether the content succeeded.
